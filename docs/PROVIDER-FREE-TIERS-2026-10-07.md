# Provider free tiers, billing and per-customer provisioning — research, 2026-10-07

Owner questions: can Rankforge lean on the free daily quotas that model providers hand out, and how do we stop money from sitting unused on a provider balance when the tutor is idle? This note records what the public pages say as of 2026-10-07 and what it means for the project. Parts A and B were written for the personal build; Part C, added the same evening after the owner clarified that the product is planned as an international commercial service, covers automatic per-customer keys, postpaid billing and detection. It extends the pricing research of 2026-10-03 in `research_notes/`; the earlier per-token tables stay valid.

Legend: **[O]** official page read directly; **[O-s]** official page seen only through a search snippet because the direct fetch was blocked; **[3p]** third-party blog, forum or aggregator. Anything marked [3p] or "not confirmed" must be re-checked in the provider console before being relied on.

## Where Rankforge stands today

The shipped tutor uses the owner's existing ChatGPT subscription through the local OAuth client. No API balance exists, so right now nothing sits idle. The project rules still apply to any adapter added from this note: no pay-as-you-go fallback on quota or auth failure, pending messages are preserved, credentials stay out of the browser, git, logs and learning history, and public-provider adapter changes stay isolated and documented.

## Part B — who holds your money, and how to keep the balance near zero

- **OpenAI.** Prepaid by default, $5 minimum, credits expire one year after purchase and are non-refundable. Auto-recharge has a threshold, an amount and an optional monthly cap, dashboard only. Hard spend limits at org and project level return 429 when hit. The complimentary data-sharing tokens require a positive balance, so some money has to sit there. [O] spend-limits guide; [O-s] help 8264644.
- **Anthropic.** Prepaid, credits expire after one year, non-refundable. Auto-reload with threshold and amount in the Console. Monthly invoicing only through Sales. Self-set spend limit under the tier cap returns 400 when hit. Rate-limit API is read-only; there is no top-up API. Minimum purchase not stated officially. [O] support 8977456; rate-limits.
- **Google Gemini API.** Postpaid by default: usage accrues on Cloud Billing and is charged at month end or at an automatically assigned spend cap, so nothing sits idle. Optional prepay exists. Tier caps pause usage; project spend caps are experimental. Cloud Billing budgets alone only alert. [O] gemini-api billing; cloud billing budgets.
- **OpenRouter.** Prepaid, $5 minimum per transaction, 5.5% card fee with $0.80 minimum, may expire unused credits after 365 days, refunds only within 24 hours. The credits endpoint reads balance and usage, per-key limits can be set daily, weekly or monthly, but the former crypto purchase endpoint now returns 410. No programmatic purchase, no postpaid. [O] terms §4.1, faq, provisioning-api-keys, crypto-api.
- **DeepSeek.** Prepaid, topped-up balance does not expire, refundable through the Billing page, 402 at zero. Minimum top-up not published. No top-up API. [O-s] FAQ.
- **Mistral.** Postpaid: pay-as-you-go is billed on consumption at month end with no upfront cost, and it is an opt-in extension beyond the plan's included usage, so leaving it off is itself a hard cap. [O-s] billing docs.
- **Groq.** Postpaid in arrears, first charges at $1/$10/$100/$500/$1,000 cumulative usage, nothing billed under $0.50, automated spending limits and alerts. Payment method required for the Developer plan. [O] billing-faqs.
- **Postpaid options found:** Google Cloud Billing, Mistral, Groq, Cloudflare Workers Paid. No aggregator was confirmed postpaid for individuals; Together and Fireworks are prepaid.
- **Programmatic top-ups.** No provider offers an official API to buy credits; OpenRouter's was removed. Auto-recharge at the smallest threshold is the only official "little by little" mechanism. Automating a card checkout ourselves would mean driving a payment page with stored card data against the providers' terms, so it is not an option for Rankforge.

## What this means for Rankforge

- **The idle-money problem is solved by billing model, not by a payment bot.** Prefer providers that bill in arrears: Google Gemini on Cloud Billing, Groq Developer, Mistral pay-as-you-go. Each has a cap that actually blocks: Google tier cap, Groq spending limit, Mistral toggle. With prepaid providers the minimum is $5, and OpenAI and Anthropic credits expire after a year, so a stuck $5 is the floor there.
- **A zero-balance tutor stack exists.** Gemini free tier for Flash or Flash-Lite with no card, Groq free plan with about 1,000 requests per day on gpt-oss-120b, Cloudflare Workers AI with 10,000 neurons per day for small models. Gemini and Mistral free modes may train on submitted text; a debrief is the learner's own English about a lab, so this is acceptable only if the learner knows and nothing private is in the prompt.
- **OpenRouter free is a $10 licence, not a balance.** 50 requests per day without purchases; a one-time $10 purchase unlocks 1,000 per day and then sits there, possibly expiring after a year.
- **Do not plan around:** GitHub Models (retired), Hugging Face free credits (gone), Cerebras and SambaNova "free" (card-gated trial or 20 requests per day), NVIDIA build (unpublished limits, prototyping terms).
- **Fit with the harness contract.** A free-tier adapter is a second provider behind the same bounded harness: the game must treat a 429 or exhausted daily quota exactly like today's subscription quota failure, keep the pending message, show the reset time and never switch to a paid route by itself. The daily reset time of each free tier becomes a visible fact in the Connection panel, not a silent retry.
- **Target market.** The product is planned as an international commercial service, so regional availability for Russia is not a selection criterion. The 2026-10-03 research already noted that Russia is absent from the OpenAI and Anthropic supported lists; that remains a fact about the founder's own access, not about the product.

## Part C — commercial frame: automatic per-customer keys, postpaid billing, detection

Owner clarification, 2026-10-07: Rankforge is planned as an international commercial service. A free tier is then not the basis for anything; the requirements become automatic provisioning of a provider-side key or sub-account for each new customer with its own cap, billing without manual prepaid top-ups, and monitoring that detects caps and failures by itself. ### What this means for a commercial Rankforge

- **Nobody offers all three primitives at once.** The only API-first "key with a dollar cap" is OpenRouter, and it is prepaid. The only places where key creation, quota overrides, budgets and cost export are all APIs and billing is postpaid are the clouds: Google Cloud, AWS, Azure. Their dollar caps are either request and token quotas or lagging budget actions, not an instant per-key dollar stop.
- **The source of truth must be our own gateway, not the provider.** Every provider returns token usage in its responses. The server-side harness that already bounds input and output per request should meter cost per customer in Rankforge's database, enforce the customer's allowance before calling the provider, and bill customers through Stripe, metered or as a wallet. Provider-side keys and workspaces per customer are hardening for blast radius and attribution, not the billing ledger. Provider usage and cost APIs are for reconciliation and alerting, with 5 minutes to 24 hours of lag.
- **Shortlist for the harness provider, by billing and limits.** First, Google Cloud with Vertex AI or a postpay Gemini billing account: postpaid, a bound API key or service account per customer by API, quota overrides by API, BigQuery export. Second, OpenRouter: the easiest per-customer key with a dollar cap and reset period, one key reaches Gemini, Claude and GPT when a mission needs a stronger model, but prepaid with a 5.5% fee, so the auto top-up threshold and the credits endpoint become the alarm. Third, AWS Bedrock: the full enterprise answer with IAM, cost tags and budget actions, at the price of more plumbing. OpenAI and Anthropic direct are project or workspace per customer, prepaid, and cannot set spend limits by API, so they are model choices behind the gateway rather than the billing layer. Groq is postpaid but has no key or usage API at all.
- **Quality is not decided by this note.** The repository holds `content/english-calibration.json` with authored debrief cases and expected verdicts. The next step for choosing a model is to run those cases through the shortlisted models with the existing grading instructions and compare verdict agreement and cost per debrief; the file itself says it is authored formative feedback, not human-calibrated ground truth, so a disagreement is a reason to look, not a score.
- **Detection and monitoring.** The gateway must treat these as provider-side stops and keep the pending message: 402 from OpenRouter, Gemini prepay and DeepSeek at zero balance; 429 with a spend-limit code from OpenAI and Mistral; 400 `blocked_api_access` from Groq; Google payment failure, which stops every project at once. Alongside: a daily reconciliation job comparing our ledger with the provider's usage or cost API, alerts on the provider balance or budget by threshold, and per-customer analytics on cost per debrief and per coaching exchange, so an unprofitable allowance is visible before the invoice.
- **Free tiers are off the table for the product.** Gemini's unpaid tier may not serve users in the EEA, UK or Switzerland and trains on submitted content; Mistral's free mode trains on data; Groq's free plan has no commercial ban but is rate-limited to a few hundred requests a day. They remain useful for development and for the owner's personal play.
- **Project rule to revisit.** `AGENTS.md` forbids a pay-as-you-go fallback. That rule protects the subscription-based personal build. The commercial build replaces it with an explicit allowance per customer enforced by the gateway; the behavior on exhaustion stays the same, pending messages are preserved and nothing silently switches route.
